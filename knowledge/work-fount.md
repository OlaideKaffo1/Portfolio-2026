## The problem

- Employees gave feedback and never saw it change anything. Managers and HR had the data, but no clear way to turn it into decisions leadership would back.
- One customer had seen employee turnover rise **28%** in two years, and **65%** of their exit interviews named "unaddressed workplace frustrations". *(Both figures are from that one customer. Always say so.)*
- Three people, one broken loop: employees spoke up, managers couldn't tell what to fix first, and HR couldn't prove the value of fixing it.

## Research

I led the research myself, remotely over Zoom, starting at INGKA and widening to other industries:
- 24 contextual interviews at INGKA across departments and levels. I ran 14 of them.
- 8 interviews at other organisations in tech, healthcare, finance and manufacturing.
- 16 sessions with HR and leadership, all facilitated by me.
- A review of survey data and exit interviews, and a competitive analysis.

**Four causes came up in every organisation:**
1. A trust gap: people didn't believe honest feedback would lead to change.
2. Feedback fatigue: long surveys, with nothing visible coming out of them.
3. Problems seen too late: managers found out once issues were critical.
4. Data, but no decisions: HR had data but couldn't turn it into priorities.

Underneath it all: feedback was treated as a survey to run, not a loop to close.

## The idea

**Turn feedback into a map of the employee journey, so anyone can see which moments matter most, and why.**

Four principles: quick to give, see what matters most, know why not just what, and impact you can prove.

**Choosing what to build first.** With Fount's decision makers, I ran a prioritisation vote on what was most feasible, most valuable and most likely to bring in revenue, then an ideation workshop on the journey for each group. Eight features made the first plan and four waited, including an AI agent. Version one shipped the core: quick pulse surveys, and a journey view that turns answers into priorities.

## Act 1 · Fount

1. **Pulse surveys in minutes.** Short surveys from templates, sent to the right people for a set time, quick to answer on any device. Before: one long annual survey with results months later.
2. **The whole journey at a glance.** An overview with the journey's score, key drivers ranked by impact, a heatmap comparing groups, and every score next to its benchmark and sample size. Before: feedback copied into spreadsheets and themes guessed.
3. **Inside one moment.** Each moment breaks down into the people and things that shaped it, what people felt, and the comments behind the numbers. Before: a slide that said "further investigation needed".

## A year later: why Fount AI

- Feedback was spread across 5 to 8 tools, 73% of HR leaders spent more time analysing than acting, and it took 6 weeks on average to spot an issue.
- I ran a second round of research over three weeks: 18 HR directors and VPs, 12 People Operations managers, 9 team leads and 4 C-suite executives.
- The journey map for Sarah, a VP of People Operations, shows the change: about twelve weeks from a problem to a fix before Fount AI, about two weeks with it. *(This is the design journey map. The measured results are in the table below.)*

## Act 2 · Fount AI

Three strategic calls shaped it:
1. **Help people decide, don't decide for them.** HR is accountable, so Fount AI asks before it acts and shows the comments and sources behind its answers.
2. **Answer "what should I do next?"** It suggests fixes for each moment and ranks them by return.
3. **Speed up the slow middle.** The longest wait was between finding a problem and acting on it. Fount AI ranks fixes by return and gives HR the reasons to take to leadership. Customers were also ready to pay thousands more for it in their enterprise deals.

Features:
4. **Ask Fount AI.** HR asks in plain words; it names the main problems, shows the comments behind them, and suggests fixes with sources.
5. **What to fix first, and why.** For each moment, the fix with the highest return, the hours and money it saves, and the reasons, so HR can defend it.

## Testing

- **Fount:** 18 employees at INGKA, plus 9 participants from other organisations and internal stakeholders. In August 2023 testers got a real task: find two or three stories in the data. People found the dashboard clear, simple and tidy. A surprise: most didn't notice the benchmarks.
- **Fount AI:** 24 people at INGKA, plus 23 from other organisations, including testers from T-Mobile, UBS, UHG and Discover. Tested on a Figma prototype. Customers asked for the biggest problems first, then the comments, and that's how Fount AI answers, starting with the top five moments.

## What this shows about how I work

- I design the loop, not the survey.
- I add AI where the data shows a need, not because AI is in fashion.
- I keep people accountable for decisions: the AI shows its evidence and asks before it acts.
- I listen across every level: two rounds of remote research, one per product.

## What's next

From the roadmap I proposed: an action tracker, a feedback portal and performance management; industry modules for high-friction industries like manufacturing; integrations with performance and learning platforms; and enterprise single sign-on. The goal: nobody should ever wonder whether speaking up was worth it.

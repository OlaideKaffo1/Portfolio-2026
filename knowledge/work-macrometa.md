## The problem

- Macrometa could do a lot, but showed new users very little of it. There was no real onboarding, the interface wasn't consistent, and support tickets were piling up on the customer success team. People dropped off, and customers left.
- The first screen after logging in was a map, empty charts and terms like "geo fabrics" and "query workers", with no next step. The sidebar was eleven all-caps links in one flat list.
- Customers said it in their own words (real messages; the customers aren't named):
  - "I have to say your user interface for this is very confusing and unhelpful."
  - "I remain frustrated I'm not able to find some of these solutions for myself using your documentation."
  - "I would love to be in the loop on what MM is doing and changing."

## Research

I led the research myself, in four ways:
- **Support feedback:** with the customer success team, I went through what customers had sent and documented the common pain points.
- **A first-time walkthrough:** I used the product as a brand-new user.
- **Interviews and a survey with developers,** including Macrometa's own engineers. Developers made up 70% of the company.
- **A competitive analysis** of onboarding at Fauna, Hasura and Confluent. Macrometa had a get-started guide, docs and tutorials, but no guided landing page, walkthrough, onboarding pointers or templates, and its copy wasn't clear. Every gap became something to design.

**My survey of 12 developers:**
- 58% wanted pointers beyond the first visit, not a one-time tour.
- 92% would share an email at sign-up, but only 25% their organisation's name or size.
- Two thirds didn't mind whether docs lived in the console or on a separate site.
- One answer asked for "a brief video of key capabilities", and the new welcome has one.

**Three insights:**
1. No real first experience: a new user's first visit didn't lead anywhere productive.
2. Nothing for every level: no tutorials, templates or guides for developers of all experience levels.
3. An inconsistent interface.

**Who it was for:** Tom, a junior engineer at a new startup who needs as much support as possible, and Alex, an engineering manager at a mid-sized company looking for tools that help the team work fast.

## The idea

**A clearer, consistent interface, and a guided start, so no developer has to figure it out alone.**

Four principles: find your way, one calm system, show the way, and start from something.

## Part 1 · A clearer product (with the Head of Design)

The bigger share of the project. Everything in Part 2 is built on it.
1. **A layout that groups the work.** The sidebar went from eleven flat links to a few groups (Activity, Data, Compute, Access, Network), and the dashboard became readable at a glance.
2. **One system, every screen.** Breadcrumbs, tabs, badges and menus that work the same way everywhere, from simple lists to the query editor and security settings.

## Part 2 · A guided start (led by me)

3. **A clear way in.** Sign-up leads with what you can build, asks only for what's needed, and lets developers sign up with GitHub or Google, with no credit card. Invited teammates get one clear button.
4. **A first visit with a next step.** New users land on a welcome with a one-line intro, a short video and three places to start. We went through two iterations of how to point people to blueprints and starting points.
5. **Never a blank page.** Creating a collection became a guided path: an empty state that teaches, plain-language choices, and sample datasets that create a working collection in one click.

## Testing

I tested key interactions with **15 developers** across experience levels before launch.

## Results

Measured with product analytics and the customer success team, **after six months of use**.

| Result | Figure |
|---|---|
| Customer retention | **+54%** (the headline number) |
| Support tickets | **−55%** (from customer success) |
| Sign-up completion | **73% faster** |
| Template adoption | **85%**, because one click added a working sample |
| User satisfaction | **+50%** |
| Sales leads | **+57%** |
| Revenue | Macrometa made **$2M** that year |

**On the $2M:** better onboarding was part of that story, and marketing promoted the new flow to bring people in. It's a result the work contributed to, not one it caused alone.

## What this shows about how I work

- I start where people get stuck.
- I turn gaps into a plan.
- I design for every level: samples and tutorials for Tom, CLI and SDK links for Alex's team.
- I share the work and own my part: the UI refresh was shared, onboarding and activation were mine.

Solving the first-step problem06 / 15
Engineering couldn't build the full assistant in the time we needed. The team's first instinct was a smaller chatbot, and that was a fair instinct. I pushed back, because a smaller chatbot is just a worse chatbot. We'd still have to design the whole conversation, it could still recommend playbooks that don't exist, people would still have to type a problem they couldn't put into words, and it wouldn't clearly tell us whether guidance helps. So I changed the question from "what's the smallest assistant we can build?" to "what's the one job that matters most right now?". The answer every time was finding the right playbook.

How we measured: product data for getting started and coming back, customer surveys and feedback for satisfaction14 / 15
We measured over four months, comparing the months before and after launch. Product data for getting started and coming back, customer surveys and the customer success team for satisfaction. New customers starting a project and a playbook went from 57% to 85%. Satisfaction went from 68% to 95%. People returning within 30 days went from about 10% to 40%, four times as many, and most come back weekly. That's the one that says the most about the design: with the right playbook from the start, people had a reason to return. And about 18% of customers who had cancelled came back to try the Recommender within two weeks of the release email. These results belong to the product and the team.
